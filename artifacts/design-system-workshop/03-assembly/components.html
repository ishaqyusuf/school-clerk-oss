School Clerk · Direction 03

Assembly

A friendly school morning. Assembly is built from the brand mark’s own colours — navy, sky, teal and amber — for bursars, registrars, teachers and parents who don’t think of themselves as software people. Every module has a colour tile, so people find their way by colour as much as by label. Cards float on soft shadows instead of borders, controls are generously rounded, buttons and badges are pills, and one amber dot — the mark’s endpoint — means “you are here”, “unread” or “this far”. Copy talks like a helpful colleague: “Good morning, Aisha”, “3 admissions waiting for you”.

Vertical or horizontal

Do & don’t

Six rules that keep Assembly from drifting back to the old monochrome, Midday-style dashboard.

Do float content on white cards with soft shadows.

Don’t box every region with 1px grey borders or add a hard header bar.

Do head every module with its colour tile, in the sidebar and on the page.

Don’t use a monochrome icon rail that only reveals labels on hover.

Do use pill buttons and fully rounded, tinted badges.

Don’t use square badges or small grey-on-grey 12px labels for status.

Do keep the navy pill as the only filled navigation state, with the amber dot.

Don’t highlight the active item with a faint grey wash or a left border.

Do save amber for “you are here”, unread and progress endpoints.

Don’t use amber for body text or big fills — it fails contrast on white.

Do write like a colleague: “3 admissions waiting for you”.

Don’t write system talk: “Records fetched”, “Entity updated”, “Submit”.
